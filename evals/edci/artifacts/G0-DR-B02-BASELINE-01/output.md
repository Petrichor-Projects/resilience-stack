# DR-B02 Baseline Output — Buyer-Language Synthesis

## Decision

Test a consultancy promise centered on turning scattered activity into a defensible weekly decision—not on supplying another dashboard. The packet supports this as the strongest working hypothesis, but five synthetic interviews cannot establish market prevalence.

## Ranked patterns

### 1. Buyers want decision clarity more than additional reporting

This is the strongest pattern because three participants describe a gap between available activity or information and the decision it should drive:

- I-01 cannot identify which work creates durable demand.
- I-02 wants someone to identify the signal that changes the plan, explicitly contrasting that need with another dashboard.
- I-04 says the team agrees on what happened but not why.

Working language to test: **“Turn scattered growth signals into the next decision your team can defend.”** This is proposed copy, not a verbatim customer statement.

### 2. The underlying pain is causal disagreement

I-01 questions which work creates durable demand; I-04 reports disagreement about why last quarter's result occurred. Together they suggest that descriptive visibility alone is insufficient—the team lacks a shared causal interpretation. This overlaps with pattern 1 but is distinct: the first is the desired outcome, while this is the diagnosis of the current failure.

### 3. Polished positioning may still collapse into feature comparison

I-03 says polished positioning does not prevent sales calls from becoming feature comparisons. This is decision-relevant but appears only once, so it should be treated as a secondary hypothesis rather than a common market pattern.

## Contradictory signal

The packet does not support a blanket “dashboards are useless” claim. I-02 rejects another dashboard because it would not identify the decision-changing signal. I-05 says a dashboard could create alignment if paired with a clear weekly decision ritual. The more defensible interpretation is that reporting has value when embedded in a decision process; the unmet need is not necessarily the absence of a dashboard but the absence of interpretation and ritual.

## Evidence limits

- The sample contains five synthetic interviews, not observed customers.
- Roles and statements are heterogeneous; the packet does not establish segment-level differences.
- Counts indicate repetition inside this packet only. They do not estimate market frequency or willingness to pay.
- The evidence does not distinguish whether buyers prefer advisory work, a productized service, software, or a hybrid.

## Next decision

Decide whether to test **decision clarity as the lead promise** for a productized diagnostic or advisory offer. Do not yet decide the delivery format or rewrite the whole positioning system.

## Smallest validation step

Run six to eight fresh problem interviews with qualified buyers who have made a recent growth decision. Ask each person to reconstruct the last decision where data existed but the team disagreed about action. Test for:

- the exact trigger and cost of delayed or disputed decisions;
- what information was available;
- why existing reporting did not resolve the choice;
- whether an external interpretation plus weekly ritual would have changed the outcome; and
- what they already pay—in time, tools, or advice—to solve it.

Advance the hypothesis only if at least four participants independently describe a recent, costly decision-clarity failure and can name a credible existing workaround or budget. Otherwise, revisit the causal-disagreement and positioning-comparison hypotheses separately.

## Method

Closed-book synthesis of I-01 through I-05 only. No web, vault, or unstated customer evidence was used.
